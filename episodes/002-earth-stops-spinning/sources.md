# Sources / numbers (simplified, for fun)
- Earth's surface speed at the equator ≈ 1,670 km/h; at 40° latitude ≈ 1,670·cos 40° ≈ 1,280 km/h. (Astronomy magazine, "What happens if Earth stops rotating?": https://www.astronomy.com/science/what-happens-if-earth-stops-rotating/)
- Speed of sound in air at 20 °C ≈ 343 m/s ≈ 1,235 km/h → 1,280 km/h is supersonic.
- Distance covered in 1 s at 1,280 km/h = 355 m (relative to a ground that has stopped).
- Fastest wind gust ever recorded (non-tornado): 408 km/h, Barrow Island, Australia, 1996 (WMO). 1,280 / 408 ≈ 3.1×.
- Air and oceans keep their eastward momentum when the solid Earth stops (Weatherzone: https://weatherzone.com.au/news/what-would-happen-if-earth-stopped-rotating/1726270 ; Science Notes: https://sciencenotes.org/what-would-happen-if-the-earth-stopped-rotating/).
- Twist: if the spin restarts at the same rate, objects still moving east at 1,280 km/h are again at rest relative to the ground (simplified; friction and impacts ignored).
