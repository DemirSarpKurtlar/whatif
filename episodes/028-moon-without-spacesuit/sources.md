# Sources / facts — 028 standing on the Moon without a spacesuit for 15 seconds
- **Don't hold your breath.** In sudden decompression the air in your lungs expands; holding it can rupture the lungs (pulmonary barotrauma). Exhaling lets it escape. NASA "Ask an Astrophysicist" answer (imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov, cited in the pages below); Scientific American: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/survival-in-space-unprotected-possible/ · Aerospaceweb: https://aerospaceweb.org/question/atmosphere/q0291.shtml
- **Ebullism: saliva boils, skin swells but holds.** Below ~6.3 kPa (Armstrong limit) body water at 37 °C boils; water vapour bubbles in tissue swell the body (up to roughly twice its volume per the NASA Bioastronautics Data Book), but skin is strong enough that the body does not burst. https://www.livescience.com/human-body-no-spacesuit · https://gizmodo.com/what-would-really-happen-if-you-were-exposed-to-vacuum-5709637
- **~10-15 s of useful consciousness.** NASA Bioastronautics Data Book (SP-3006): "some degree of consciousness will probably be retained for 9 to 11 seconds"; the 1966 NASA chamber accident subject lasted ~14 s. https://scienceblog.com/a-real-vacuum-exposure-records-contradict-almost-everything-hollywood-has-taught-about-it-bodies-do-not-explode-blood-does-not-boil-out-through-the-skin-and-people-do-not-freeze-or-black-out-instantl/
- **1966 NASA vacuum chamber accident (Jim LeBlanc, Houston, 14 Dec 1966).** Suit hose came loose; he felt the saliva on his tongue start to bubble, blacked out after ~14 s, chamber repressurised in ~87-90 s and he recovered fully. https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/447311-highest-equivalent-altitude-exposure-survived · https://www.livescience.com/human-body-no-spacesuit
- **~90 s survivable.** 1965 Brooks Air Force Base study: dogs exposed to near vacuum (1/380 atm) for up to 90 s all survived; 2 min or more was often fatal. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/survival-in-space-unprotected-possible/ · https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2012/06/you-can-survive-being-exposed-to-the-near-vacuum-of-space-for-about-90-seconds-with-no-longterm-damage/
- **Lunar surface temperatures.** Equatorial daytime ~120 °C (≈121 °C), night/shadow down to ~-170 °C (Space.com: as cold as -173 °C; LRO Diviner). With no air there is no convection, so heat leaves or enters the body only by radiation and contact: you would not cook or freeze within 15 s (evaporative cooling of moist skin and mouth actually chills them). https://www.space.com/14725-moon-temperature-lunar-days-night.html · https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/lrolitho7temperaturevariation27may2014.pdf · https://www.livescience.com/space/the-moon/what-temperature-is-the-moon
- Simplifications: exact second-by-second timings vary per person; the "seconds" in the video are a dramatised timeline of the documented order of effects. Survival "within ninety seconds" is based on animal data and one human accident — "probably", not guaranteed.
