[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if every insect on Earth vanished tomorrow? [pause] At any moment, about ten quintillion insects are alive. That's over a billion for every person. Overnight, they're all gone. At first, it feels nice. No mosquitoes, no flies. But by spring, something is missing. About three quarters of the world's main food crops rely, at least partly, on animal pollinators, mostly insects. Apples, almonds, cocoa, coffee... harvests crash. Then the food chain starts to fall. Most songbirds feed insects to their chicks. Frogs, lizards, bats and many fish all go hungry. [leaning in] And the quietest problem? Insects help recycle the dead. Fallen leaves, dung and carcasses start to pile up, and the soil slowly loses its nutrients. [building tension] Rice, wheat and corn don't need insects, so we would survive. But the world would be grey, silent... and hungry. Would you miss the mosquitoes?
