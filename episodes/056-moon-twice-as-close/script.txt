[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if the Moon were twice as close? [pause] At one hundred ninety-two thousand kilometres, it looks twice as wide, and four times bigger in the sky. [leaning in] But tides pull eight times harder, because tidal force grows with distance cubed. A two-metre tide becomes sixteen. Every thirteen hours, the sea floods into coastal cities, then drains away. The Moon now circles Earth in under ten days. Total solar eclipses jump from less than one a year to about ten, lasting up to half an hour. Full moons shine four times brighter. [building tension] And Earth's spin brakes sixty-four times harder. Would you still live by the sea?
