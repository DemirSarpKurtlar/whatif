# Sources — 058 What if you didn't sleep for 11 days?

Real case: Randy Gardner, 17, San Diego high-school student, science-fair experiment over the 1963/64 winter break
(started 28 Dec 1963, finished January 1964). Observed by Lt. Cmdr. John J. Ross (US Navy medical) and sleep researcher
William Dement (Stanford). Numbers are rounded as spoken in the narration.

## Record: 264.4 hours = 11 days 24 minutes (1964)
- Guinness World Records, "What's the limit to how long a human can stay awake? And why we don't monitor the record" (2023):
  Gardner's 264.4 h (11 days 24 min) in 1964; Guinness no longer monitors/accepts sleep-deprivation attempts because of the health risks.
  https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/whats-the-limit-to-how-long-a-human-can-stay-awake-733188
- NPR, "Lessons from sleeplessness: The 60th anniversary of Randy Gardner's world record" (28 Jan 2024): 17-year-old, 264.4 h,
  Dement + Ross observing; Gardner: "the mental part is what went downhill"; he developed insomnia decades later.
  https://www.npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274/sleep-deprivation-record
- Scientific American, "How Long Can Humans Stay Awake?": by the end Gardner was "awake" but cognitively dysfunctional;
  altered states / numerous brief episodes of overwhelming sleep (microsleep).
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-long-can-humans-stay/

## Hour 17 — impairment like 0.05% blood alcohol
- Williamson & Feyer (2000), Occup Environ Med 57:649-655, PMID 10984335: after 17-19 h without sleep, performance on some
  tests was equivalent to or worse than at a BAC of 0.05%; longer wakefulness reached ~0.1%.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1739867
  https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/infrastructure-transport-vehicles/road-transport-infrastructure/safety/publications/2000/Fatig_Alc
  (Caveat: only some tests, lab measures — narration says "your reactions match", which fits the reaction-speed tests.)

## Day 2 — microsleeps
- Sleep Foundation: microsleep = sleep episodes of ~15 seconds or less, often with eyes open and without the person noticing;
  main cause is sleep deprivation. https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-deprivation/microsleep
- Cleveland Clinic: "your eyes may stay open, but your brain essentially checks out".
  https://health.clevelandclinic.org/what-you-should-know-about-microsleep
- (Day 2 is our narrative placement; microsleeps are documented throughout long deprivation incl. Gardner's — SciAm above.)

## Days 3-4 — moodiness, clumsiness, memory, hallucinations, paranoia
- Ross's reports (via IFLScience / Now I Know summaries): by day 3 moody and uncoordinated, trouble with short-term memory;
  by day 4 hallucinations, delusions (he believed he was a famous pro football player), very short attention span; paranoia
  reported around days 3-4. https://iflscience.com/a-boy-went-without-sleep-for-an-11day-school-project-and-hallucinations-kicked-in-60518
  https://nowiknow.com/no-sleep-until-a-week-from-friday/
- Dement: analytical ability, perception, motivation, memory and motor control were all affected (NPR above).

## Afterwards — slept ~14 hours
- Gardner slept a little over 14 hours straight after ending the record, then returned to normal sleep within days
  (IFLScience / Now I Know above; also widely reported in Dement's accounts).

## Guinness stopped recognising the record
- Guinness stopped accepting/monitoring sleep-deprivation records (late 1990s) because of the danger (Guinness 2023 article above).

## Safety note
- The video carries an explicit "don't try this" line in narration and on screen; sleep deprivation is dangerous (driving, microsleeps).
