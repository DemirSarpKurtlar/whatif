[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if Earth had rings like Saturn? Scale Saturn's rings down to our planet, and they would start fifteen hundred kilometres up, and end eight thousand kilometres above your head. Yet they'd be only about ten metres thick. [pause] From the equator, you'd barely see them. Just a razor-thin line, straight overhead. Travel north, and that line opens into a giant glowing arch across the southern sky. From New York, it rises twenty-six degrees above the horizon. [leaning in] But go past sixty-four degrees north, and the rings sink below the horizon. You would never see them at all. At night, they keep reflecting sunlight, so midnight glows like dusk. In winter, their shadow falls across your half of the planet, making winters even colder. And any satellite orbiting inside them? Shredded. Where would you want to live, to see them best?
