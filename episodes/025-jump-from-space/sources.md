# Sources / facts — 025 jumping from the edge of space
- Felix Baumgartner, Red Bull Stratos, 14 Oct 2012: exit altitude 38,969.4 m (≈ 39 km); FAI-ratified max vertical speed 1,357.6 km/h = Mach 1.25; speed of sound reached after 34 s, top speed after ~50 s; free fall 4 min 20 s (often quoted 4:19); vertical free-fall distance 36,402.6 m. https://www.redbull.com/us-en/red-bull-stratos-release-mission-data · https://www.techradar.com/news/world-of-tech/red-bull-stratos-final-data-reveals-baumgartner-hit-mach-1-25-1129573 · https://avweb.com/news/stratos-team-updates-mission-numbers/
- Alan Eustace, StratEx, 24 Oct 2014 over Roswell, New Mexico: 135,890 ft (41.42 km, Guinness 41,422 m), about 2.5 km higher than Baumgartner; ~822 mph (≈ 1,320 km/h, Mach 1.23), sonic boom heard on the ground; free fall ≈ 4.5 min (with a stabilising drogue), ~15 min total descent. https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/107932-highest-manned-balloon-flight · https://www.livescience.com/48449-skydiver-goes-supersonic-in-record-breaking-near-space-dive.html · https://www.space.com/27536-supersonic-near-space-dive-video.html
- Armstrong limit ≈ 18–19 km (63,000 ft in the US Standard Atmosphere): ambient pressure ≈ 6.3 kPa, water boils at 37 °C, so exposed body fluids (saliva, tears, lung moisture) boil without a pressure suit. Script says "the spit on your tongue would boil" (not blood: blood pressure keeps blood in the vessels liquid). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_limit
- Air pressure at 30 km ≈ 1/100 of sea level; at 39 km even less → no wind noise, nothing to push against early in the fall. https://www2.oberlin.edu/faculty/swojtal/SFWpage/161Stuff/161Lect10/tsld007.htm
- Temperature: International Standard Atmosphere is −56.5 °C from 11 to 20 km (the layer where the thicker air slows the jumper); press reports gave about −70 °F (≈ −57 °C) for Baumgartner's jump. Above 20 km the air is actually a little warmer. https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/Standard_atmosphere · https://www.universetoday.com/articles/capsule-and-skydiver-ready-for-record-setting-freefall
- Kármán line: FAI boundary of space at 100 km, so 39–41 km is less than halfway to space. https://www.popsci.com/where-does-space-begin/
