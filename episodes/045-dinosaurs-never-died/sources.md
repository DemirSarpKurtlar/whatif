# Sources / fact notes — 045 What if the asteroid had missed the dinosaurs?

## The impact (Chicxulub, Yucatán, Mexico)
- ~66 million years ago an asteroid hit the Yucatán Peninsula (crater ~180-200 km across), marking the Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) boundary.
  Schulte et al. 2010, *Science* review: the impact triggered the mass extinction. https://oro.open.ac.uk/27298
- Impactor size: usually given as ~10 km (estimates roughly 10-15 km; Phys.org: "about 6 miles / 9.7 km"). Script: "a rock about ten kilometres wide".
  https://phys.org/news/2015-02-impact-dinosaurs.amp · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_impactor
- Extinction: "the extinction of the dinosaurs and around three-quarters of all living species" — Morgan et al. 2022, *Nature Reviews Earth & Environment*.
  Other sources say 70-80%; script: "about three quarters of all species". https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022NRvEE...3..338M
  · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous%E2%80%93Paleogene_extinction_event

## How long dinosaurs ruled
- Non-avian dinosaurs: first appear ~230-240 million years ago (Late/Middle Triassic), gone at 66 Ma → roughly 165 million years
  (the commonly cited round figure; depends on the start date). Script: "ruled for a hundred and sixty-five million years" — "ruled" is loose,
  they only became dominant in the Jurassic; the 165 My is their total span. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur
- Timeline graphic: 230 MYA → 66 MYA (gold) vs 66 MYA → now (ink), drawn to scale (164 : 66).

## Mammals at the time
- Most Mesozoic mammals were small (shrew/rat-sized); the biggest known reached badger size or a bit more
  (Repenomamus ~12-14 kg, Early Cretaceous; Didelphodon ~2.4-5.2 kg, Late Cretaceous). Nature News & Views notes the "rat-sized, nocturnal" picture
  is a simplification. Script: "mostly small, rat to badger sized". https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-03170-7.pdf
  · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repenomamus · https://www.livescience.com/57169-ancient-mammal-had-dinosaur-chomping-bite.html
- Survivors after the impact were small (≤ house-cat size); some mammals "no larger than badgers" found shelter. Mammals then radiated and grew large in the Paleogene.
  https://www.livescience.com/animals/how-did-animals-survive-the-asteroid-that-killed-the-dinosaurs · https://www.amnh.org/explore/videos/dinosaurs-and-fossils/mammals-asteroid-impact

## Birds are dinosaurs
- Birds are living theropod dinosaurs (the only dinosaur lineage that survived the K-Pg extinction).
  https://australianmuseum.net.au/The-living-dinosaurs · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_birds
- Number of species: IOC World Bird List 13.2 = 11,001 extant species; 14.1 = 11,032; its successor AviList (2025) ≈ 11,131.
  (Older counts said ~10,000 — taxonomy keeps splitting species.) Script: "over eleven thousand species".
  https://leesbird.com/2024/05/03/the-ioc-world-bird-list-14-1/ · https://www.worldbirdnames.org/new/ioc-lists

## The dinosauroid (speculation, clearly framed)
- Dale Russell & Ron Séguin, 1982, *Syllogeus* 37: "Reconstruction of the small Cretaceous theropod Stenonychosaurus inequalis and a hypothetical dinosauroid".
  A thought experiment: what might a big-brained troodontid have become if it had survived? Russell later said he nearly didn't publish it;
  many palaeontologists call the humanoid body plan too anthropomorphic. Script: "Dale Russell even imagined a big-brained dinosauroid".
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Russell · https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/troodon-sapiens-thoughts-on-the-dinosauroid

## The speculation (questions, not claims)
- "Would they ever grow big? Would primates, or humans, ever appear?" and "who would be watching this video right now?" are open questions.
  Nobody knows; mammals did diversify a little in the Late Cretaceous, but large mammals only evolved after the dinosaurs were gone.
