# Sources / math — 034 What if you fell into Jupiter?
- Arrival speed: anything falling in from far away reaches roughly Jupiter's escape velocity at the cloud tops, 59.5 km/s
  (NASA NSSDC Jupiter fact sheet: 59.5 km/s vs Earth 11.19 km/s; JPL quick facts 59.54 km/s). Check: v = sqrt(2GM/R) =
  sqrt(2 · 6.674e-11 · 1.898e27 / 71,492 km) = 59.53 km/s → "sixty kilometres a second" (≈ 214,000 km/h).
  https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/jupiterfact.html · https://sci.esa.int/web/solar-system/-/35651-jupiter ·
  https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916115614/http://www.jpl.nasa.gov/galileo/jupiter/jupiter.html
- Galileo probe (released by the Galileo orbiter) entered on 7 Dec 1995 at ≈ 47.4 km/s (170,700 km/h, 106,000 mph; slower than
  59.5 because it entered prograde with the spinning atmosphere), peak deceleration ≈ 228 g, the heat shield glowed at
  ≈ 15,500 °C ("blazing like a meteor"). It transmitted for ≈ 58 minutes (57.6 min) and fell ≈ 150 km (sources quote 150–180 km
  below the entry level) before the signal was lost at ≈ 22–23 bar and ≈ 153 °C ("twenty-two times Earth's", "hotter than boiling water").
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/galileo-jupiter-atmospheric-probe · https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/missions/galileo-probe/in-depth ·
  https://astro.sci.muni.cz/pub/galileo/first_science_summary.html · https://cosmoquest.org/x/365daysofastronomy/2009/10/19/october-19th-when-galileo-fell-into-jupiter
- No surface: Jupiter is mostly hydrogen and helium; the gas gradually becomes denser with depth (no solid ground to land on).
  https://www.space.com/the-universe/jupiter/how-can-jupiter-have-no-surface-a-dive-into-a-planet-so-big-it-could-swallow-1-000-earths
- Cloud decks, top to bottom: ammonia ice (≈ 0.5–0.9 bar, white zones), ammonium hydrosulfide NH4SH (≈ 1–2 bar, brownish;
  made of ammonia + hydrogen sulfide → "smells like rotten eggs"), water/ice clouds (≈ 3–7 bar).
  https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_Jupiter · https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01608 ·
  https://www.climate.ox.ac.uk/node/2708191
- Depth of the water cloud: hydrostatic + adiabatic estimate (μ = 2.3 g/mol, g = 24.8 m/s², T ≈ 166 K·p^0.3) gives 5 bar ≈ 50 km
  and 22 bar ≈ 125 km below the 1-bar level (≈ 150 km below the probe's entry level) → "fifty kilometres down" / "one hundred and fifty kilometres".
- Lightning: Galileo imaged lightning that appears to originate at the level of the water clouds; Juno also found shallow
  "ammonia-water" lightning higher up.
  https://www.missionjuno.swri.edu/science-findings/juno-discovers-shallow-lightning-above-jupiters-water · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.12361 ·
  https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/08/200805160926.htm
- Liquid then metallic hydrogen: deeper, hydrogen becomes a hot dense fluid (supercritical, "liquid") and at ≈ 1–3 million bar,
  roughly 10,000–25,000 km down (estimates vary), it becomes liquid metallic hydrogen, which drives Jupiter's magnetic field.
  https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/173806 · https://www.llnl.gov/news/high-pressure-hydrogen-sheds-light-jupiter ·
  https://livescience.com/61430-what-is-metallic-hydrogen.html · https://www.windows2universe.org/jupiter/interior/J_int_structure_liquid.html&edu=high
- Simplifications: "you" are imagined in an indestructible suit; the descent is stylised and time-compressed; depths are measured
  from the cloud tops. Note: NSSDC/science.nasa.gov pages were not directly fetchable from this session; their numbers were
  confirmed via search snippets and mirrors (ESA, JPL archive).
