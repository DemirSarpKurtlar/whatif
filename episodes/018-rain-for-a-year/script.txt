[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if it rained everywhere on Earth for a whole year? [pause] Not a storm. Just a steady drizzle, one millimetre an hour, every hour, on every city, desert and mountain. That sounds gentle. But by December, that's almost nine metres of water, nine times what Earth normally gets in a year. Within weeks, the ground can't soak up any more. Rivers burst their banks. Basements, then subways, fill up. On the hills, waterlogged soil starts to slide. With clouds blocking the Sun all year, the planet gets darker and colder, and most crops rot in the fields. [leaning in] But the driest places on Earth do something strange. The Atacama, the Sahara... they bloom. Seeds that waited for decades finally wake up, and the deserts turn green. [building tension] Where would you wait out the year?
