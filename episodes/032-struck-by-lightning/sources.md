# Sources / math — 032 struck by lightning
- Typical flash: ~300 million volts, ~30,000 amps; bolt heats the air to ~50,000 °F (≈ 27,800 °C, commonly rounded to ~30,000 °C / 30,000 K).
  NWS "How Powerful Is Lightning?" https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-power · NOAA JetStream FAQ https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/lightning/frequently-asked-questions · NSSL Lightning FAQ https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/faq/
- Sun's visible surface (photosphere) ≈ 5,772 K (≈ 5,500 °C): 30,000 / 5,500 ≈ 5.4 → "about five times hotter". Vaisala: https://www.vaisala.com/en/how-hot-lightning
- Duration: a return stroke is completed in a few tens of microseconds (AMS Glossary "return stroke") https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/return-stroke/ → "thirty microseconds" used as a typical value.
- Survival: almost 90% of lightning strike victims survive; ~10% die, mostly from cardiac arrest. CDC https://www.cdc.gov/lightning/about/index.html · https://www.cdc.gov/lightning/faq/index.html
  The heart's own pacemaker often restarts it after lightning-induced arrest (Cooper, AMS paper) https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/135809.pdf
- Mechanisms: direct strike only ~3-5% of injuries; side flash 20-30%; ground current 40-50% (most). Environment Canada https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/lightning/safety/dangers.html · NWS "Five Ways Lightning Strikes People" https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-struck
- Flashover: much of the current can travel over the outside of the body (skin/wet clothing) rather than through it (Cooper, AMS paper above).
- Warning signs: hair standing on end. If you can hear thunder, you are within striking distance; lightning can strike more than 10 miles (16 km) from the rain. 30-30 rule: flash-to-bang ≤ 30 s (≈ 10 km at 343 m/s) → shelter; wait 30 min after the last thunder.
  NOAA JetStream Lightning Safety https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/lightning/lightning-safety · NWS brochure https://www.weather.gov/media/hun/outreach/brochures/LightningSafetyBrochure.pdf
- Roy Sullivan (Shenandoah NP ranger) survived 7 recorded strikes, 1942-1977 — Guinness record https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/incredible-story-of-man-who-survived-being-struck-by-lightning-seven-times-733932 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Sullivan
