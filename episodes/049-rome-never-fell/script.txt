[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if the Roman Empire never fell? [pause] In four seventy-six, the West collapsed. At its peak: sixty million people, a fifth of humanity. Four hundred thousand kilometres of roads. Eleven aqueducts feeding one city of a million. Concrete so tough the Pantheon's dome still stands unreinforced, nineteen centuries later. [leaning in] Then the wild part: a Greek gear computer that predicted eclipses, and a spinning steam engine, sixteen centuries before a useful one. [building tension] Instead, Rome shrank to maybe thirty thousand people. Unbroken? Maybe steam trains by the year one thousand. Or maybe it just crumbles slower. Would you live in a Rome that never fell?
