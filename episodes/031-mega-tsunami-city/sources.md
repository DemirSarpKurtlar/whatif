# Sources / math — 031 What if a 100-metre mega-tsunami hit your city?

## Facts used
- **Deep-ocean speed ≈ jet airliner.** NOAA: in the deep ocean tsunamis "can move as fast as a jet plane, over 500 mph (800 km/h)"; near shore they slow to ~30-50 km/h while the height grows.
  - https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/tsunamis/tsunami-propagation · https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/tsunami.html
- **Math (shallow-water wave speed v = √(g·d))**, g = 9.81 m/s²:
  4,000 m → 198 m/s = 713 km/h · 4,300 m (mean Pacific) → 739 km/h · 5,000 m → 222 m/s = 797 km/h ≈ 800 km/h.
  At 10 m depth → 36 km/h; 20 m → 50 km/h (matches NOAA's "speed of a car" at the shore).
- **The sea pulls back first (drawback) — the natural warning sign.** In 2004 the sea receded minutes before the wave; people walked out to collect stranded fish.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami · https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/tsunami.html
- **2004 Indian Ocean: ~30 m.** Surveys at Lhoknga west of Banda Aceh measured ~31 m above sea level from vegetation damage (35 m flow height, 51 m hillside run-up maximum).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami · https://www.eri.u-tokyo.ac.jp/KOHO/PANKO2005/openhouse/webposter/tsuji2.pdf
- **2011 Tōhoku: ~40 m run-up** (40.5 m at Miyako, Iwate; survey 40.4 m) and **up to 10 km inland** in the Sendai area (NOAA; survey literature gives >5 km as the typical inundation on the Sendai plain — "could push ten kilometres inland" is the upper bound).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami · https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120913074725/http:/www.tsunami.gov/events/11Mar2011factsheet.php · https://link.springer.com/article/10.5047/eps.2012.02.007
- **Lituya Bay, Alaska, 9 July 1958: run-up 524 m** (trees stripped to 524 m on the headland opposite the rockslide). The wave itself was ~150 m (scale model, Fritz et al.); 524 m is how high the water ran up the slope.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Lituya_Bay_earthquake_and_megatsunami · https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/benchmarks-july-9-1958-megatsunami-drowns-lituya-bay-alaska/
- **100 m vs a 30-storey building:** ~3.3 m per storey × 30 = 99 m ≈ 100 m.

## Simplifications
- A 100 m wave at a city coast is hypothetical (would need a huge landslide, volcanic collapse or asteroid); it's scaled up from the real events above.
- The wave is drawn as one steep wall for clarity; real tsunamis usually arrive as a fast, rising flood/bore of several waves.
