[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if the Moon disappeared tonight? It's a clear night on the beach. The full Moon lights up the sand and the water. [pause] Then it's gone. For one point three seconds, you can still see it. That's how long its last light takes to reach you. Then the sky goes dark. A full Moon is over a hundred times brighter than starlight, so suddenly the Milky Way appears. By morning, the tide comes in weaker. Only the Sun pulls on the oceans now, so tides shrink to about a third. Coral reefs that spawn by moonlight lose their calendar. [leaning in] But the biggest change is slow. Today, the Moon holds Earth's tilt steady, near twenty-three degrees. Without it, over millions of years, that tilt could swing wildly, bringing brutal seasons. Where would you want to be when it disappears?
