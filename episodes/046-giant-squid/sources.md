# Sources / math — 046 What if you met a giant squid in the deep ocean?

## Facts used in the script
- **Depth ~300-1,000 m:** giant squid (Architeuthis) are thought to live at roughly 300 m to over 1,000 m (from trawled specimens and sperm-whale dive data; their exact depth range isn't known). Sunlight is close to gone by ~200-300 m (twilight zone). The script says "past three hundred metres, and the sunlight is gone" (simplified).
  - https://www.bibalex.org/SCIplanet/en/Article/Details.aspx?id=12382
  - https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/7QGlzYR8lzFstQ
- **Eye 27 cm** (~10.6 in): Britannica says the eye can be up to 27 cm across, among the largest of any living animal and second only to the colossal squid's. Other sources give ~25-30 cm (some claim up to 38 cm), so 27 cm is a conservative figure. A dinner plate is ~25-28 cm. Human eyeball ≈ 2.4 cm, so the squid's eye is ≈ 11× wider (python).
  - https://www.britannica.com/animal/giant-squid
  - https://www.elasmo-research.org/education/evolution/g_squid.htm
- **"Biggest eyes of any animal, along with the colossal squid":** the colossal squid's eye measured ~27 cm on a partly collapsed specimen (probably 30-40 cm in life). The two squids hold the record together.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_squid
- **Length up to ~13 m:** Britannica gives ~13 m (43 ft) as the maximum total length of examined specimens, including the two long feeding tentacles; claims of 18 m+ are unconfirmed. ReefQuest: ~13 m females, ~10 m males.
  - https://www.britannica.com/animal/giant-squid
  - https://www.elasmo-research.org/education/evolution/g_squid.htm
- **"As long as a city bus":** most single-deck city buses are ≤ 12 m long (12 m is one of the most common urban sizes). 13 / 12 = 1.08 → "as long as" (python). The size plaque draws both at the same scale (13 m vs 12 m).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-deck_bus
  - https://karsan.com/en/blog/e-mobility/different-bus-sizes-in-urban-transportation
- **First filmed alive in its deep habitat in 2012, off Japan:** July 2012, about 15 km (nine miles) east of Chichi-jima, Ogasawara Islands; National Museum of Nature and Science (Tsunemi Kubodera), NHK and Discovery Channel, from a submersible at depths of up to ~900 m; aired January 2013. (A giant squid had been filmed alive at the surface in 2006, but not in its habitat.) Second habitat footage: June 2019, Gulf of Mexico, ~100 miles SE of New Orleans, NOAA expedition, Medusa camera (Edith Widder, Nathan Robinson, Sönke Johnsen), at ~750+ m.
  - https://press.discovery.com/us/dsc/press-releases/2013/giant-squid-captured-video-its-natural-habita-2236
  - https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/giant-squid-caught-live-screen
  - https://www.marinebio.org/giant-squid-caught-on-film-in-its-natural-habitat-for-the-first-time/
  - https://wtop.com/animals-pets/2019/06/giant-squid-captured-on-camera-for-the-first-time-in-the-us/
- **Sperm whales = its main predator; sucker scars:** circular scars on sperm whale skin (around the mouth and head) come from the suckers on giant squid arms and tentacles as the squid fights the whale's jaws. Sperm whales dive to 1 km+ to feed on them.
  - https://ocean.si.edu/node/108265 (Smithsonian Ocean, "Giant Squid Sucker Marks")
  - https://www.livescience.com/dead-sperm-whale-rotting-australia.html
- **Kraken:** Norse sea-monster legend, made famous by Bishop Erik Pontoppidan's *Natural History of Norway* (1752-53), based on fishermen's tales. Many think sightings of giant squid inspired it, which is why the script says "It **may** be the monster sailors called the Kraken."
  - https://snl.no/kraken
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraken
  - https://www.amnh.org/explore/ology/ology-cards/285-kraken
- Context (on-screen small line): heavier relative = colossal squid, largest specimen 495 kg (Antarctic, 2007).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_squid
