[calm, intriguing documentary narrator, steady energy] What if every volcano on Earth erupted at once? There are about one thousand three hundred and fifty potentially active volcanoes on land. [pause] Imagine all of them waking up in the same second. The biggest ash columns punch more than twenty kilometres into the sky. Inside them, static builds until the clouds crackle with lightning. Within hours, the jet stream spreads the ash around the planet. In two thousand ten, one small Icelandic volcano grounded a hundred thousand flights. Now, every airport on Earth closes. [leaning in] But the real danger is invisible. Sulfur gas turns into a reflective haze high in the stratosphere. In eighteen fifteen, a single volcano, Tambora, cooled the world so much that eighteen sixteen became the year without a summer. Snow fell in June. Crops failed. Now multiply that by hundreds. Would you rather face the lava, or the long winter after?
