# Sources / fact notes — 055 What if woolly mammoths came back?

## The last mammoths: Wrangel Island, ~4,000 years ago
- The last known woolly mammoths lived on Wrangel Island (Arctic Ocean, off NE Siberia) until roughly 4,000 years ago. Their extinction was "fairly abrupt" and
  came without signs of an earlier population decline. Earliest human traces on the island date to ~3,650-3,350 years ago, i.e. after the mammoths.
  Dehasque et al. 2024, *Cell* (21 genomes, 14 from Wrangel): founded by at most ~8 animals, then ~200-300 individuals until the end; inbred, but inbreeding did not doom them.
  https://www.sciencenews.org/article/last-woolly-mammoths-species-extinct · https://www.livescience.com/animals/mammoths/mystery-random-event-killed-off-earths-last-woolly-mammoths-in-siberia-study-claims
  · https://www.sci.news/paleontology/extinction-wrangel-island-mammoths-07671.html

  Asian elephant cells (the Asian elephant is the mammoth's closest living relative). It is NOT a clone and not an exact copy.

## Elephant pregnancy
- Asian elephant gestation 18-22 months; African elephants ~22 months: the longest of any land mammal (a 2023 paper suggests bowhead whales may be longer).
  https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/70491-longest-gestation-period-mammal · https://www.ifaw.org/international/journal/animals-with-long-gestations

## Size: "six-tonne giants"
- Woolly mammoth males: shoulder height ~2.7-3.4 m, weight up to ~6 t (estimates range ~4-8 t); females smaller. Roughly African-elephant size.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_mammoth · https://www.dimensions.com/element/woolly-mammoth-mammuthus-primigenius

## Pleistocene Park and the permafrost
- Pleistocene Park (Sergey & Nikita Zimov, near Chersky, NE Siberia) keeps high densities of horses, bison, reindeer, musk oxen etc.
  Beer et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports* "Protection of permafrost soils from thawing by increasing herbivore density": trampling halved the mean snow depth
  and lowered mean annual soil temperature by ~1.9 °C; models suggest large herds could slow permafrost thaw (projections depend on assumed animal numbers).
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-60938-y (doi 10.1038/s41598-020-60938-y) · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32184407/ · https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/03/200317103836.htm · https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/packing-the-tundra-with-animals-could-slow-arctic-melt/
  · https://www.sciencealert.com/releasing-herds-of-animals-in-the-arctic-could-help-fight-climate-change-says-study
- Caveat: a 2022 Frontiers study of grazing sites (incl. the park) found mixed carbon effects; more research is needed.
  https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2022.893478/pdf
- Script: "grazing herds trampled the snow, cooling the soil almost two degrees". The HUD counter shows -1.9 °C (yearly average).
- Northern permafrost soils hold ~1,460-1,600 billion tonnes of carbon, about twice the carbon in the atmosphere.
  https://arctic.noaa.gov/?p=629 · https://phys.org/news/2008-09-permafrost-carbon-content.html
- Script: "frozen permafrost holds twice the carbon in our air". This is why mammoth fans (incl. Colossal) pitch them as tundra "ecosystem engineers" — speculative.
