# Sources — 057 What if Yellowstone's supervolcano erupted?

All numbers in the script, captions and plaques, with where they come from. Primary source: USGS Yellowstone Volcano Observatory (YVO).

| Claim (as used) | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Huckleberry Ridge eruption, first and largest caldera-forming eruption | ~2.1 million years ago, ~2,450 km³ (USGS: "more than 2,450 km³") | USGS YVO, Summary of Yellowstone eruption history — https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/science/summary-yellowstone-eruption-history |
| Mesa Falls eruption (shown on the timeline) | ~1.3 million years ago, ~280 km³ | same USGS page; USGS FAQ "How much volcanic activity has there been at Yellowstone since the most recent giant eruption?" — https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-much-volcanic-activity-has-there-been-yellowstone-most-recent-giant-eruption |
| Lava Creek eruption (last super-eruption, formed today's Yellowstone Caldera) | 631,000 years ago (older USGS pages round to ~640,000); ~1,000 km³ | USGS YVO Caldera Chronicles, "Yellowstone's magmatic system over the past 631,000 years" — https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/yellowstones-magmatic-system-over-past-631000-years ; Matthews et al. 2015 (Lava Creek Tuff 40Ar/39Ar age 631.3 ka) |
| Mount St. Helens 1980 for scale | ~1 km³ of ash | USGS image "Yellowstone eruption volume comparison" — https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/yellowstone-eruption-volume-comparison |
| Ash fall from a super-eruption would reach much of the US, bull's-eye pattern | decimetres to metres in the northern Rockies, centimetres in the upper Midwest (Minneapolis, Des Moines), millimetres on the coasts | Mastin, Van Eaton & Lowenstern 2014, G-Cubed, "Modeling ash fall distribution from a Yellowstone supereruption" — https://www.usgs.gov/publications/modeling-ash-fall-distribution-a-yellowstone-supereruption ; press release https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/08/140827122624.htm |
| "Least likely scenario: much of the United States, southern Canada and northern Mexico would experience some ashfall" | — | USGS YVO, Volcanic hazards at Yellowstone — https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/science/hazards |
| Volcanic winter: sulfur aerosols could cool global climate by "a few degrees" for years | estimates range from ~1.5 °C (Timmreck et al. 2010) to several °C in older models; USGS says the exact effect cannot be predicted | USGS YVO, Questions about supervolcanoes — https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/questions-about-supervolcanoes ; EarthSky summary of supervolcano cooling studies — https://earthsky.org/earth/supervolcanoes-major-cooling-study/ |
| Magma reservoir is mostly solid crystal mush | about 5–15% melt (shallow reservoir); deeper reservoir ~2% melt | USGS FAQ "How big is the magma chamber under Yellowstone?" — https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-big-magma-chamber-under-yellowstone ; YVO "Yellowstone's magma reservoir comes into sharper focus" — https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/yellowstones-magma-reservoir-comes-sharper-focus |

| Annual probability of another caldera-forming eruption | about 1 in 730,000 (0.00014%) per year, based on past history | USGS YVO, Questions about supervolcanoes — https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/questions-about-supervolcanoes |
| More likely future events | lava flows (rhyolite or basalt; none for ~70,000 years) and hydrothermal (steam) explosions, small ones every few years | USGS FAQ "What type of eruption will Yellowstone have if it erupts again?" — https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-type-eruption-will-yellowstone-have-if-it-erupts-again ; USGS Fact Sheet 2005-3024 "Steam explosions, earthquakes, and volcanic eruptions — what's in Yellowstone's future?" — https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2005/3024/ |

Notes on wording (anti-fearmongering): the script says "could cool", "much of the United States", gives the yearly odds explicitly and ends with the more likely outcomes. The end card states the current alert level framing ("NO SIGNS OF A SUPER-ERUPTION") which matches YVO's routine status reports (alert level NORMAL / aviation GREEN).
