# Sources (simplified, for fun)
- If all glaciers and ice caps melted, global sea level would rise ~70 m (230 ft) — USGS FAQ: https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-would-sea-level-change-if-glaciers-melted
- Antarctic ice sheet ≈ 58 m sea-level equivalent; Greenland ≈ 7 m (IPCC AR5 WG1 ch.4 / BAS). Antarctica holds ~90% of Earth's ice.
- Floating sea ice does not raise sea level when it melts (already displacing its weight of water).
- Statue of Liberty: ground to torch 92.99 m; ground to top of pedestal 46.94 m — NPS: https://www.nps.gov/stli/historyculture/statue-statistics.htm
  → +70 m water (above ~3 m ground) reaches roughly two-thirds up the 34 m figure: chest level.
- ~40% of the world's population lives within 100 km of the coast — UN Atlas of the Oceans / UN Ocean Conference fact sheet.
- Freshwater input dilutes North Atlantic surface water and weakens the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (Gulf Stream system) that keeps NW Europe mild — e.g. NOAA / IPCC AR6.
- A real full melt would take thousands of years (~5,000 yrs at current trends per National Geographic 2013 "Rising Seas" map).
