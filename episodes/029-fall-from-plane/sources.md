# Sources / facts — 029 falling out of a plane at cruising altitude
- Cruising altitude: airliners typically cruise at ~33,000–42,000 ft (≈ 10–12.8 km); script uses 11 km. International Standard Atmosphere temperature from 11 to 20 km is −56.5 °C (real values vary, often −50 to −70 °C); air pressure at 11 km ≈ 22% of sea level. https://flywestwind.org/Training/Advanced/ISA.php · https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/Standard_atmosphere
- Time of useful consciousness (FAA AC 61-107B table): 30,000 ft ≈ 30 s–1 min; 35,000 ft ≈ 30–60 s, roughly halved (15–30 s) after a rapid decompression. Script: "about thirty seconds before you black out". https://migflug.com/afterburner/far-91-211-how-long-can-you-fly-without-oxygen-hypoxia/ · https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_61-107B.pdf
- Belly-to-earth terminal velocity near the ground ≈ 120 mph ≈ 195 km/h (≈ 54 m/s); FAI rounds to ~200 km/h. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_skydiving · https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/skydiving-how-fast-can-you-fall-through-the-air-terminal-velocity
- Vesna Vulović, flight attendant on JAT Flight 367, 26 Jan 1972, near Srbská Kamenice (now Czech Republic): Guinness record "highest fall survived without parachute", 10,160 m (33,333 ft), inside a section of the wreckage that came down on a snowy, wooded mountain slope; she was badly injured (coma, fractures) and recovered. Guinness still lists the record (a 2009 journalistic claim that the plane broke up much lower was not accepted). https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/highest-fall-survived-without-parachute · https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2022/4/how-vesna-vulovic-survived-the-highest-fall-ever-with-no-parachute-697786 · https://thisdayinaviation.com/26-january-1972/ · https://www.cbsnews.com/news/vesna-vulovic-stewardess-who-miraculously-survived-jet-crash-in-1972-dies/
- Popular Mechanics, "How to Fall 35,000 Feet — And Survive" (2010) describes the same scenario (black out in thin air, regain consciousness lower down, ~3 min to impact; snow, trees, slopes and wreckage as the factors in documented survivals). Mirrors could not be fetched from this session; summarised from search snippets. https://www.thestranger.com/blogs/2010/02/12/3415775/how-to-survive-falling-from-an-airplane-seriously · https://www.todayifoundout.com/?p=11520 (157 known survivors of falls from crashing planes, 1940–2008)
- Math (python, /tmp/fall029.py, ISA density, drag ∝ ρv², sea-level terminal velocity 54 m/s, start 10.7 km at rest):
  - terminal velocity at ~10.7 km ≈ 349 km/h (thin air); you pass 10 km after ≈ 13 s at ≈ 300 km/h, peak ≈ 305 km/h → "fifteen seconds in, three hundred km/h";
  - the thickening air then slows you: 6 km ≈ 270 km/h (61 s), 3 km ≈ 230 km/h, 1 km ≈ 205 km/h, impact ≈ 196 km/h → "brakes you to two hundred";
  - total fall time from 10.7 km ≈ 156 s (2.6 min), from 11 km ≈ 160 s → "almost three minutes";
  - after ~1 min you are near 6 km, where the air is thick enough that consciousness can return (TUC at 18,000–22,000 ft is 5–30 min) → "a minute later, you wake up" (hedged: individual, not guaranteed).
