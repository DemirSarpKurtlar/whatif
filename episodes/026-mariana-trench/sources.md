# Sources / math — 026 What if you sank to the bottom of the Mariana Trench?
- Challenger Deep (southern Mariana Trench) is ≈ 10,935 m deep (2021 survey figure; older/other surveys range ≈ 10,900–11,000 m) → "almost eleven kilometres down".
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_Deep · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Trench
- Pressure at the bottom ≈ 1,086 bar (15,750 psi), more than 1,071× sea-level atmospheric pressure → "over a thousand times stronger than at the surface".
  https://www.ucl.ac.uk/culture-online/case-studies/2021/apr/how-much-pressure-builds-deepest-point-ocean · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Trench
- "A car balanced on your fingernail": 1,086 bar = 108.6 MPa = 10,860 N per cm² ≈ 1,107 kg-force on each square centimetre (a fingernail is roughly 1 cm²; a small car ≈ 1,000–1,200 kg). 15,750 psi = 15,750 lb ≈ 7.1 tonnes on every square inch.
- Light: the sunlight zone ends ≈ 200 m (blue light only; the "twilight zone" runs 200–1,000 m); below ≈ 1,000 m no sunlight reaches at all (midnight/bathypelagic zone), water ≈ 4 °C (39 °F).
  https://www.whoi.edu/know-your-ocean/ocean-topics/how-the-ocean-works/ocean-zones/ · https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/the-5-layers-of-the-ocean.html
- Bottom water in the Challenger Deep ≈ 1–4 °C (a 2017 mooring measured 2.45 °C at the seafloor).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Trench · https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/images/content/30-2_dziak_S2.pdf
- Titanic wreck lies ≈ 3,800 m (12,467 ft) deep in the North Atlantic (NOAA Ocean Exploration).
  https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ · https://www.worldatlas.com/oceans/how-deep-the-titanic-wreck-actually-sits.html
- Mount Everest = 8,848.86 m (China–Nepal survey, Dec 2020). 10,935 − 8,849 = 2,086 m → its summit would still be ≈ 2 km underwater.
  https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall · https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/12/8/mt-everest-nepal-china-announce-revised-height-at-8848-metres
- Life at the bottom: shrimp-like amphipods (e.g. Hirondellea gigas, ~5 cm, eat sunken plant debris), sea cucumbers and giant single-celled xenophyophores were filmed in the Challenger Deep.
  https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/deepest-point-on-earth-life · https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/deep-knowledge/ · https://earthsky.org/science-wire/vibrant-mix-of-marine-life-found-at-extreme-ocean-depths/
- Time to get there (for scale, not narrated): Trieste (1960) took ≈ 4 h 48 min to descend; James Cameron's Deepsea Challenger (2012) ≈ 2 h 36 min.
  https://history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nmusn/explore/prior-exhibits/2020/trieste/project-nekton.html · https://www.csmonitor.com/layout/set/amphtml/Science/2012/0326/Why-James-Cameron-was-forced-to-surface-early
- Simplification: "you" are imagined in a suit that survives the pressure; the video is a stylised descent (time compressed).
